# Twillory editorial Facebook Pages

Brandon Wasoski owns the day-to-day workflow. Coordinate with Jack when his Meta access, passkey, or authority to accept commercial terms is needed. Use Twillory-owned styling Pages with distinct names and audiences. Identify Twillory as the operator in the public bio or About section, for example, “Workwear styling ideas from Twillory.” Avoid implied independent coverage, invented editors or readers, and fabricated outside reviews or testimonials.

## Start with the existing Pages

1. Confirm the line, launch scope, deliverables, and whether Brandon wants to resume existing Pages or build a new batch. Use established scope when it is already clear.
2. Open Twillory’s portfolio in Meta Business Suite and check Settings → Pages. Verify the portfolio and Page before any write. Keep live identifiers and links in the private handoff.
3. For the women’s handoff, start with Nine to Nine (workwear), Wheels Up Wardrobe (travel), and Worn on Repeat (capsule styling). For each Page, check operator disclosure, bio, profile photo, cover, Brandon’s Page access, and past and scheduled posts in Planner. Check ad-account access before preparing ads. Log what is verified and what is pending. Resume missing work on existing Pages and check for duplicates before creating a Page or scheduling a replacement post.
4. For a new batch, assign each Page one niche, buyer, and styling angle. Search Facebook for each candidate name and avoid names that could be confused with existing clothing Pages. Use the Website category when available. Keep the bio human and within the visible field limit, with the Twillory disclosure.

## Select products and verify claims

- If Brandon supplies a product list, check PDP status and current inventory in Shopify. When selecting from data, shortlist around five to six live, in-stock products per Page from the relevant Shopify collection and validate demand against the authorized BigQuery product performance view or another verified sales source covering the line since launch. Flag out-of-stock and oversold products. If access is missing, note which checks are pending before selecting products for ads.
- Verify claims against the current Twillory PDP and creative rulebook. Use current product or campaign photography approved for this work. Preserve source links in the private handoff.

## Build the Page kit

- Create a **1080 × 1080** profile image with a solid brand color, a serif wordmark, an italic accent, and a simple mark tied to the niche. Use Cormorant Garamond and Jost when available.
- Create a **1640 × 856** cover with a left wordmark and tagline panel and two photography panels. Keep text and frames within approximately y=150–706 and at least 60 px from the sides. Preview crops at desktop and mobile widths and use adequate headroom.
- Prepare **1080 × 1350** seed post images. Use a two-up or 2 × 2 layout for color or outfit comparisons. Give each Page its own images.
- Save one folder per Page containing `1 - Profile picture.png`, `2 - Cover photo.jpg`, `Captions.txt`, and numbered `Posts/Post NN.jpg` assets. Match each caption to its image filename or mark it text-only. Include a combined caption reference and verify the delivered image files against the preview.

## Create or update Pages and seed Planner

1. Get authorization for the public changes in scope. For new Pages, use Settings → Pages → Add → Create a new Facebook Page in the verified Twillory portfolio. If Meta requests reauthentication, hand control to the signed-in account holder. If Meta asks to accept terms on behalf of Twillory, obtain explicit approval from the authorized approver before acceptance. Use the Business Suite Page ID from Settings if the composer needs it.
2. Draft approximately 10 posts per Page, including one or two text-only posts. Write practical outfit or styling content. Keep editorial posts free of prices, reviews, “we tested” claims, unsupported statistics, and promises of a future series. Apply Twillory’s creative rulebook, including its restrictions on collar, dryer, and wrinkle-free claims.
3. Use the approved start date and confirm the timezone displayed by Planner. For a fresh batch of 10 posts, use two per day for three days and one per day for four days. Stagger times across Pages. For an existing Page, account for the posts already in Planner and fill gaps only.
4. Use Business Suite or an authorized integration. If the session cannot upload the approved image, prepare the composer and hand it to Brandon for upload and scheduling. Review the Page, image, caption, date, time and timezone before submitting. Read the post back in Planner after scheduling. After a timeout or reset, inspect Planner before retrying. Get confirmation before deleting duplicates.
5. Report each Page’s status, scheduled count, verified times and timezone, and pending items. Use the original one-week seeding period as the planning default before proposing an ad test. Confirm Page and ad-account access and launch authorization before activating ads. Use the Twillory static-ads workflow and creative rulebook if statics are in scope.
